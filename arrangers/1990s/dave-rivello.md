His sectional writing emphasizes block scoring in the Basie tradition, where the entire brass section moves in rhythmic unison with independent saxophone counterlines providing textural contrast. Soli passages employ five-part saxophone voicings with the baritone doubling the lead alto at the octave below, creating full-bodied section sound without muddiness. Instrumental combinations follow proven formulas: trombones in tight close position for shout choruses, trumpets voiced in fourths for modal passages, and saxophones utilizing thickened lines with added tensions for harmonic richness. Contrapuntal writing focuses on call-and-response patterns between brass and saxophone sections, with occasional two-part counterpoint between lead trumpet and lead alto creating melodic interplay. Register treatment is conservative but effective: lead trumpet operates primarily in the staff (written G4-C6), trombones work their comfortable middle range, and saxophones exploit their most resonant registers. Rhythmic devices include swing eighth-note interpretation with strategic anticipations, section kicks on off-beats, and pyramid dynamics where sections enter sequentially on successive beats. Textural approaches alternate between full ensemble tutti passages and stripped-down rhythm section breaks, creating dynamic contrast through orchestral reduction. Rivello favors the standard 5-4-4 configuration with optional fifth trumpet for high-note specialists. His dynamic architecture builds through accumulated section entrances culminating in fortissimo shout choruses, followed by sudden drops to piano for contrast. The signature technique involves guide-tone voicings where inner voices move by step while outer voices leap, creating smooth harmonic motion with traditional big band impact.
